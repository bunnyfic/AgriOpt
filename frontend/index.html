AI crop diagnosis

Crop disease detection & treatment

Select the crop and the part shown, then upload a clear photo of its leaf, fruit, flower, stem, root, or whole plant. The AI will look for likely diseases or pests and suggest treatment options for the selected crop.

Choose a crop supported by the Crop recommendation system.

Select the part with visible symptoms so the AI can focus its diagnosis.

Drop a photo of the affected crop part or click to choose JPG, PNG or WebP

This is an AI estimate, not a confirmed diagnosis or prescription. For serious or spreading problems, ask a local agricultural extension officer. Only use treatments approved for this crop and problem in your area, and follow the product label.

Take a better photo

  • Get close and fill the frame with the affected crop part.
  • Use daylight. Avoid flash and harsh shadows.
  • Hold steady so the leaf is sharp, not blurry.
  • Show the symptoms and some healthy tissue, if possible.
  • One crop per photo. If unsure, try a few clear views.

    Using treatments safely

    • Use a medication only if it is approved for this crop and problem locally.
    • Follow the product label exactly; never guess a dose or mix products.
    • Wear the protective equipment listed on the label.
    • Keep pesticides away from people, animals, food and water.
    • Dispose of infected plant material according to local guidance.

    The dataset contains soil and climate readings used to train the crop recommendation model, along with the crop label. Its inputs are nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), temperature, humidity, soil pH, and rainfall.

    Dataset credit: eonyushi on Kaggle.

    Crop disease detection

    AI image analysis

    The disease page sends the selected crop photo to the configured AI vision provider for an image-based diagnosis. The crop recommendation dataset above contains no crop disease image labels and is not used to train that diagnosis.

    No separate disease image dataset or dataset credit is documented in this project. AI results are estimates and should be checked against local agricultural guidance.
